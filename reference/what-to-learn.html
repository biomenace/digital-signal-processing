This page is the answer, sorted by how many of them want each subject — so the top of the page is what to learn first, and the bottom is what to learn only if you pick that particular direction. The institutions themselves, ranked and with their notes, are on the companion page.

How to use this in a minute

  1. Read Tier 1 only. That is the syllabus, and it does not depend on where you go.
  2. Pick a country with the filter row, or a direction from Tier 3, and re-read. Chips that do not belong to that country disappear and the counts follow.
  3. Part 2 says how deeply each subject has to be known — on paper, in code, or well enough to ask about it. That is what decides how long it takes.

Part 2 How well you have to know each of them

The same subjects again, this time as the level of mastery the handbooks and job ads imply — what has to work on paper, what only has to work in code, and what just has to be recognised well enough to ask the right question.

by handon paper, in an exam, without a computer in codePython or MATLAB, on real data recogniseenough to follow it and ask about it

Linear algebra

  • by handMultiply matrices, solve a small linear system, invert a 2×2, get eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a 2×2 or 3×3 on paper.
  • in codeCovariance matrices, SVD and eigendecomposition with numpy on real recordings.
  • recogniseWhy CSP, PCA and state-space analysis are all the same eigenproblem in different clothes.

BCCN prep-course lists "vectors, matrices and systems of linear equations"; TU Berlin's BCI module names linear algebra as the background it expects.

Probability + statistics

  • by handDistributions, expectation and variance, Bayes' rule, and deriving a maximum-likelihood or MAP estimator for a simple model.
  • in codeHypothesis tests, permutation tests, cross-validated error bars on a real dataset.
  • recogniseWhich test fits which experimental design, and what a p-value does not tell you.

Tübingen CN05 asks students to "derive basic machine techniques using maximum likelihood or maximum a posteriori estimation"; Oldenburg psy220 evaluates with cross-validation and permutation tests.

Feature extraction + classification

  • by handThe LDA decision rule, a confusion matrix, and what regularisation does to a covariance estimate.
  • in codeThe whole pipeline end to end: window the signal, build features, train, cross-validate, report honestly.
  • recogniseAdaptive and unsupervised classifiers — enough to say why a model decays between sessions.

TU Berlin assesses by portfolio (5 practicals, 10 assignments, 2 written tests); Oldenburg's second half is a seminar implementing BCI methods on a real dataset.

Python

  • in codenumpy, SciPy, scikit-learn, PyTorch — plus PySide6/Qt if you want the FAU thesis topics; MATLAB where the group is MATLAB-based.
  • by handWrite working code under exam conditions: Oldenburg's psy240 exam is 120 minutes of writing analysis scripts.
  • recognisegit, an environment that reproduces, and a plot someone else can read.

FAU n-squared ads name "solid Python (numpy, PyTorch)" verbatim; Tübingen CN05 accepts Python or R.

Fourier transform + complex numbers

  • by handComplex arithmetic and Euler's formula; the DFT sum written out; the spectrum of a sine, a rectangle and a shifted signal.
  • in codeFFT with windowing, band power as a feature, spectrogram of an EEG or EMG segment.
  • recogniseFrequency resolution versus window length, and why leakage appears.

BCCN's prep-course item 3 is "complex numbers and Fourier transform"; Oldenburg psy220 builds features from "Fourier transform, spectral estimation techniques".

DSP

  • by handThis is the one place where hand calculation is demanded outright: convolve short sequences, take the z-transform of a difference equation, place poles and zeros, judge stability, compute a frequency response, and work out a sampling rate and its aliases.
  • in codeFIR/IIR filtering, correlation, ARMA models, artefact removal on a real biosignal.
  • recogniseWhere the sampling theorem bites in practice — electrode bandwidth, anti-alias filters, downsampling.

Magdeburg's Digital Information Processing states the objective as "mathematically explain the modus of operation", assess stability, and "calculate the frequency response and reconstruction of analogue signals".

PCA / ICA / dimensionality reduction

  • by handOn a toy 2-D cloud: centre it, write the 2×2 covariance matrix, get its eigenvectors, and say which direction is PC1 and how much variance it holds. That is the level asked for — not a hand-solved 64-channel decomposition.
  • in codePCA and FastICA on real EEG or EMG: choose the number of components, remove an artefact, decompose motor units.
  • recognisePCA = variance and orthogonality; ICA = statistical independence; ICA's scale and order are arbitrary, so components must be identified, not assumed.

BCCN's Machine Intelligence covers "PCA, kernel-PCA, ICA and blind source separation (Infomax, FastICA)"; Magdeburg repeats PCA/ICA/k-means/EM in Theoretical Neuroscience II.

Calculus / analysis

  • by handDerivatives and the chain rule, gradients of a multivariable function, Taylor expansion to second order, standard integrals.
  • in codeGradient-based optimisation — enough to read a training loop rather than trust it.
  • recogniseLocal minima, conditioning, and why a learning rate matters.

Tübingen CN02 asks for "basic knowledge of analysis and linear algebra"; BCCN's prep-course opens with "calculus of functions in one real variable".

ODEs and dynamical systems

  • by handSolve a linear ODE, find fixed points, linearise around them, sketch a phase portrait and call the stability.
  • in codeIntegrate the Hodgkin-Huxley or integrate-and-fire equations numerically and reproduce a spike.
  • recogniseWhat a bifurcation is and where it shows up in a neuron model — the courses present these, they do not ask you to prove them.

BCCN's prep-course item 4 is "differential equations: linear and nonlinear, stability"; Tübingen CN02 is built on linear and nonlinear dynamical systems.

Information theory

  • by handEntropy and mutual information of a small discrete distribution, computed on paper.
  • in codeMutual information between stimulus and response on spike or EEG data, with the bias that small samples introduce.
  • recogniseFisher information as a bound on decoding accuracy — recognise the argument, do not re-derive it.

FAU's Computational Neurotechnology expects you to "apply information theory for the description of neural activity"; Magdeburg's Theoretical Neuroscience I covers Fisher and Shannon information.

Muscle → machine

  • in codeThe full EMG chain: filter, segment, extract features, classify a gesture, then recalibrate across days.
  • recogniseMotor units and how the CNS grades force; why surface EMG smears what intramuscular EMG separates.
  • by handNothing beyond the signal maths already listed — this tier is judged by what you have built.

FAU's INS module covers "control of prosthetic devices from EMG signals in amputees"; the n-squared thesis ads ask for a working pipeline in Python.

Brain → machine

  • in codeCSP plus a linear classifier on motor-imagery EEG; ERP averaging; an online loop that does not lag.
  • recogniseThe paradigms and what each costs the user: c-VEP, SSMVEP, P300, ERD/ERS, error-related potentials.
  • by handDesign an experiment: trials, timing, controls, and what would make the result meaningless.

TU Berlin's module runs from ERPs and spatial filters to CSP and adaptive classifiers; DONUT's ten projects are all paradigm plus decoder.

Event detection + source localisation

  • in codeDetectors for spikes and seizures, band-limited features around 80–600 Hz, evaluated against clinical labels.
  • recogniseForward versus inverse problem, head and source models, why the inverse is ill-posed. Nobody expects you to derive an inverse solution.

Freiburg's brain-signals module does detection first, then modelling to localise the generators.

Hardware

  • by handRead a schematic, choose an amplifier and its filters, lay out a small PCB, handle electrodes and impedance.
  • recogniseMicrofabrication and silicon probes at conversation level — that is a lab's work, not a portfolio project.
  • in codeFirmware or embedded processing far enough to get a sampled signal off the board.

Magdeburg teaches Advanced PCB Design and Analog and Mixed-Signal Systems in the same English programme; Freiburg's silicon probe module is a lecture course, not a lab you can join from outside.

Transfer learning

  • in codeTrain on one session or subject, test on another, and measure the drop honestly before you fix it.
  • recogniseWhy calibration decays — electrode shift, skin, fatigue, attention — and which adaptation each argues for.

FAU advertises "Unsupervised Cross-Day Calibration for HD-EMG Hand Gesture Decoding"; DONUT DC7 names "deep transfer learning" for exoskeleton control.

Reinforcement learning, control, robotics

  • by handAn MDP on a tiny grid: value iteration or a few Q-learning updates by hand.
  • in codeTD or actor-critic on a toy environment; a controller in the loop with a simulated device.
  • recogniseModel-based versus model-free, and why medical devices rarely learn online on a patient.

BCCN's Machine Intelligence covers MDPs, value/policy iteration and Q-learning; FAU's Artificial Motor Learning is model-based RL and learning control.

Neurophysiology background

  • recogniseAction potential, motor unit, synapse, the main EEG rhythms, and enough neuroanatomy to read a methods section.
  • by handExplain where a measured signal physically comes from — no derivation, but no hand-waving either.

Freiburg's Neuroscience for Engineers is exactly this, and Oldenburg's psy170 pairs it with hands-on EEG recording.

Estimation / Kalman filtering

  • by handDerive a scalar Kalman filter once — predict, update, gain — and know why the gain moves the way it does.
  • in codeFuse EMG with an IMU or encoder into one state estimate, and show it beats either signal alone.
  • recogniseEKF, UKF and particle filters as choices — what breaks when the plant is nonlinear.

Ottobock's control lead ad asks for “Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF), Extended Kalman Filter (EKF), particle filters, or equivalent Bayesian estimation methods” by name; UCLouvain teaches Kalman and particle filtering inside its signal-processing course.

Biomechanics + musculoskeletal modelling

  • by handFree-body diagram of a joint, moment arms, and why a Hill-type muscle is a spring, a damper and an activation delay.
  • in codeDrive an OpenSim-class model from recorded EMG and compare predicted torque against measured torque.
  • recogniseInverse vs forward dynamics, gait phases, and what motion capture actually measures.

MIT names “biomechanics” in its one-line applicant profile; Twente teaches “controller design from a biomechanical model”; KTH's work is optimal control over musculoskeletal models.

Stimulation encoding + psychophysics

  • by handCharge per phase, charge balance and the safety limits — the arithmetic that decides whether a stimulus is allowed at all.
  • in codeFit a parameter-to-percept curve from a subject's reports, with the statistics that a subjective report demands.
  • recogniseWhy a percept drifts — adaptation, electrode change — and what biomimetic encoding is trying to reproduce.

Pittsburgh maps electrode, amplitude, frequency and pattern onto a reported percept and keeps it stable for years; Case Western publishes on sensory adaptation to electrical stimulation; Aalborg closes the loop with electrotactile feedback.
